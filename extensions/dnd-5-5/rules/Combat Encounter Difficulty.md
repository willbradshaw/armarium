# Combat Encounter Difficulty

A combat encounter's difficulty compares the XP of its creatures with the
party's XP budget.

## Difficulty

- **Low.** The encounter is likely to have one or two scary moments, but the
  characters should win with no casualties. Some might need to use healing
  resources. As a rough guide, a single monster is a low-difficulty challenge
  for four characters whose level equals its Challenge Rating.
- **Moderate.** Without healing and other resources, the encounter could go
  badly. Weaker characters might be taken out of the fight, and there is a slim
  chance that a character dies.
- **High.** The encounter could be lethal for one or more characters, who will
  need smart tactics, quick thinking and maybe some luck to survive.

## XP budget

number by the number of characters in the party. Five level 3 characters have a
moderate-difficulty budget of 225 × 5 = 1,125 XP.

## Spending the budget

Each creature is worth the XP in its stat block, which follows from its
Challenge Rating (CR). Add creatures, subtracting each one's XP from the
budget, and spend as much of it as you can without going over; a few XP left
over is fine. Four level 1 characters have a low-difficulty budget of 200 XP,
which buys one Bugbear Warrior (200 XP), two Giant Wasps (100 XP each), or six
Giant Rats (25 XP each, 150 XP in all).

The XP is awarded for defeating a creature in combat or otherwise neutralizing
it. A creature summoned by a spell or other magical ability is worth the XP in
its stat block unless a rule says otherwise.

## Troubleshooting

- **Many creatures.** The more creatures, the greater the risk that a lucky
  streak deals more damage than expected. With more than two creatures per
  character, include fragile ones that can be defeated quickly, especially for
  characters of level 1 or 2.
- **Absent players.** A player's absence might warrant removing creatures to
  keep the intended difficulty. Creatures can also flee, or reinforcements
  arrive, to adjust an encounter during play.
- **CR 0 creatures.** Use these sparingly, particularly those worth 0 XP. For
  many CR 0 creatures in one encounter, use swarms instead.
- **Number of stat blocks.** More than two or three stat blocks in one
  encounter can be hard to run, especially for complex creatures.
- **Powerful creatures.** A creature whose CR is higher than the party's level
  might take out one or more characters with a single action. An Ogre (CR 2)
  can kill a level 1 Wizard with one blow.
- **Unusual features.** Consider leaving out a monster with a feature that
  lower-level characters can't easily overcome when the characters' level is
  below its CR.
